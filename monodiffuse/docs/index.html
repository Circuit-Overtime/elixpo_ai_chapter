Native Binary-Weight Training Outperforms Zero-Shot Sign Projection in MNIST Diffusion

Ayushman Bhattacharya, Anwesha Chakraborty, and Nihal Gazi

Read the PDF Zenodo DOI Code and artifacts

Abstract

Does severe failure after projecting a diffusion model to one-bit weights show that its binary representation is unusable? In a controlled small MNIST residual U-Net, native W1A32 training obtains domain-feature Fréchet distance 46.33 ± 17.24, 28.4-fold lower than matched zero-shot projection at 1314.20 ± 314.96, while remaining substantially worse than FP32 at 3.15 ± 0.38. The result is deliberately local: optimizing binary forwards reaches a much better solution than this projection baseline, but it does not establish full W1A1 success, floating-point parity, or transfer to natural images.

Status

This manuscript is a preprint and has not undergone peer review. A separate compact CIFAR-10 attempt is excluded from the evidence because its FP32 control also failed.
